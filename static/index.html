Rules and evaluation

20 weighted signals help you decide what to ask next. They cannot establish who is behind an offer.

How the score works

Each rule counts once. The total is capped at 100: 0–17 means fewer signals, 18–44 means clarify details, and 45–100 means pause and verify. This score is not a probability.

Nearby “do not” and “jangan” clauses can suppress a match. This simple heuristic can miss context, sarcasm, negation and phrasing in either language. Ordinary payroll wording can trigger the account rule.

An experimental second reading

A small supervised text model is trained on 34 synthetic offers and evaluated on 16 from different scenario families. Translations stay together. Its label does not change the rules result. Sparse examples and unfamiliar words limit it.
